# Virginia Reservoirs LTREB Data Publication Authorship Guidelines

## Aim
The aim of this document is to outline expectations for authorship on Environmental Data Initiative (EDI) data products within the Virginia Tech Reservoir Group. Our goal is to be inclusive of all potential collaborators who have provided meaningful contributions, without whom the final data product would not have been possible. We encourage data product leads to discuss authorship early in the evolution of a project and continue to check in about authorship at project milestones (e.g., at the transition from data collection to data QA/QC). It is our expectation that the project lead and coauthors will work together to document their contributions – both at the initiation of and throughout a research activity that is expected to result in a data product – in a formal authorship contribution statement in the data product metadata. We hope this document will inspire discussion among our group members and collaborators throughout the life cycle of our data products.

***

## EDI data products
Publishing data products is a new practice for most scientists. Our mandate as part of our NSF-supported Virginia Reservoirs Long-Term Research in Environmental Biology (LTREB) project is that the data we collect will be published with metadata to receive a DOI (digital object identifier) in the Environmental Data Initiative (EDI) repository within a year of collection. Data publishing is now required by many journals and funding agencies, and is a best practice for reproducible, open science. 

We recognize that there are many contributions to the collection, analysis, QA/QC, collation, and publishing of data. Our philosophy is that coauthorship on a data product requires independent, intellectual contributions to the data product (see examples of qualifying contributions below). In many cases, the authors of a data product will be different from the authors on manuscripts derived from the data product, as the authorship contributions to the data product vs. manuscript will likely vary.

For the core Virginia Reservoirs LTREB monitoring program data products, the expectation is that the program principal investigator will be the lead and corresponding author, in recognition of the substantial contribution of maintaining and funding the continuity and integrity of our EDI data products over time. In cases when a data product is produced solely outside of the LTREB’s core monitoring program (e.g., as part of a Ph.D. dissertation chapter), authorship will be handled following the best practices outlined in this document, with the team member who led the data product serving as lead and corresponding author.

Because of the substantial amount of work needed to publish data for the core LTREB monitoring data products, we have developed a practice in which the Reservoir Group member who is serving as the "data product lead" becomes the default second-listed author on that year's data product DOI revision. Here, we define a data product lead as the primary point person for a data product throughout a year. For some data products, there may be co-data product leads, which would be described in the authorship contribution statement.

The remaining author list includes the collaborators who contributed in other ways to the data product to merit coauthorship that year. To merit coauthorship, the collaborators need to contribute in at least two distinct and substantial ways (including, but not limited to): consistent fieldwork throughout the field season for the routine monitoring data collection (e.g., sampling at the reservoirs or preparing field gear most weeks in the summer and contributing in the fall/winter/spring as able); leading sensor/instrument maintenance/deployment; leading field crew; initiating new data streams and workflows; leading QA/QC; maintaining data workflows, including sensor connectivity; in-lab chemical or microscopic analyses; data analyses (including substantial digitization and processing); and publishing the data. Collaborators who assisted with the data product but did not meet the criteria for coauthorship will be listed in the data product acknowledgements. 

The data product lead and coauthors will be updated for each data product every year. The one exception is when a data product is first published, in which case all present and past data product leads and collaborators who have made contributions meriting coauthorship should be included as coauthors in that specific year.

An authorship contribution statement describing each coauthor’s contributions during that year will be included within the "Notes" section of the EDI data product, similar to authorship contribution statements on journal articles. As an example, "CCC developed and coordinated the monitoring program of these water quality variables. ABP led instrument maintenance and data collection and was the point person for this data product; ZYX led data QA/QC and substantially contributed to fieldwork." The previous years’ data product leads should be listed in the data product acknowledgements, which will be appended to over time.

These guidelines apply to all Reservoir Group members (faculty, postdocs, technicians, graduate students, undergraduates) and emerged from iterative discussions within our group about data product authorship over several years. Because the practice of data publishing is rapidly changing, we revisit and revise this section annually to enable these guidelines to evolve over time, supplemented by recurring conversations about authorship throughout the year. We generally discuss authorship contributions for the annual publishing of core Virginia Reservoirs LTREB monitoring program data products during the fall and early winter before January EDI data publishing workshops. If you have any questions about data product authorship, we encourage you to reach out to the Reservoir Group faculty leads for clarification. If a broader discussion is needed, faculty will organize and facilitate a full Reservoir Group team meeting to reach consensus. If consensus cannot be reached, the Virginia Reservoirs LTREB program's principal investigator takes ultimate responsibility for final authorship decisions.
